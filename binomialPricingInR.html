Binomial Options Pricing with R

Objective

Explore Binomial trees in option pricing using R

Binomial experiment

Stock prices are said to following a random walk -i.e flipping a coin.

Each successive coin toss represents a binomial trial with two outcomes “success” or “failure”.

If a coin is tossed twice \(X = ({H, T})^2\) the probability of getting a head \(H\) is denoted by \(p\) and the pronbability of getting a tail \(T\) is denoted by \((1 - p)\).

The number of successive tosses is denoted by \(n\) which follows a discrete process. The binomial probability distribution of \(X\) is given by:

\[ f_{X} = \sum_{k=1}^{n} p^k (1-p)^{n-k} \]

In this case we have \(n = 2\), the probability of success is \(0.5\).

Binomial trees

Binomial trees are a technique to model the movement of stock prices. Its a graphical representation of the possible paths the stock price can take over the life of the option. Each step is represented by an a probability of an up movement or down movement.

Consider successive coin tosses as share prices - share price \(S_{0} = \$100\), - the strike price is \(K = \$100\), - the risk free rate is \(r = 2.5\%\), \(t = 0, 1, 2\) - The upwards and downwards movement of the share is \(u = 1.0488\) and \(d = 0..9747\)

We want to calculate the outcome of the share price at each step, so;

  • At \(t = 0\)
    • we have \(S_{0} = \$100\)
  • At \(t = 1\)
    • \(S_{u} = uS_{0} = (1.0488)(100) = 104.88\)
    • \(S_{d} = dS_{0} = (0.9747)(100) = 97.47\)
  • At \(t = 2\),
    • we have \(S_{uu} = uuS_{0} = \$110\)
      • computed as \((1.0488)*(1.0488)*(100)\)
    • \(S_{ud} = udS_{0} = \$102.44\)
      • computed as \((1.0488)*(0.9747)*100\)
    • \(S_{dd} = ddS_{0} = \$94.96\)
      • computed as \(0.9747 * 0.9747 * 100\)

binomialTree = function(s, delta_t, u, d, N){
  tree = matrix(0, nrow = N+1, ncol = N+1)
  
  sigma = log(u) / sqrt(delta_t)
  sigma = log(d) / sqrt(delta_t)
  
  for(i in 1:(N+1)){
    for(j in 1:i){
      tree[i, j] = s * u^(j-1) * d^((i-1) -(j-1))
    }
  }
  return(tree)
}

Options pricing

Consider the purchase of a European Call option with a strike of \(K = \$100\) The current price is \(\$100\) with an expiration date T. - If stock price at expiry is less than \(\$100\) we do not exercise. - If the stock price is greater than \(\$100\) then we exercise.

Suppose the share price is greater than \(\$100\)

The risk neutral probability of an up-move is;

\[ q = \dfrac{(1+r)\Delta t - d}{u - d} \]

Suppose continuously compounding rate;

\[ q = \dfrac{e^{r\Delta t} - d}{u - d} \] The risk neutral probability of a down-move is \((1-q)\) where \(\Delta t = \dfrac{T}{N}\)

The price of a European call option \(C_{0}\) and the price of a European put option \(P_{0}\) is given by;

\[ C_{0} = (e^{-r\Delta t})^N \sum_{k=0}^{N} \dfrac{N!}{k!(N-K)!}(S_{0}u^k d^{N-k} - K) + q^k(1-q)^{N-K} \]

\[ P_{0} = (e ^{-r\Delta t})^N \sum_{k=0}^{N} \dfrac{N!}{k!(N-K)!}(K - S_{0}u^k d^{N-k}) + q^k(1-q)^{N-K} \]

Compute the risk neutral probability

\[ q = \dfrac{(1 + 0.05) (\dfrac{2}{2}) - 0.98}{1-0488 - 0.9747} = 0.6788 \]

We can compute this using;

q_prob = function(r, delta_t, u, d){
  X = (1 + r) * delta_t
  q = (X * delta_t - d) / (u - d)
  return(q)
}

q_prob(
  r = 0.025,
  delta_t = 1,
  u = 1.0488,
  d = 0.9747
)
[1] 0.6788124

So, our risk neutral probability \(q\) is 0.6788124

Cox-Ross-Rubinstein (CRR) model

CRR_model_call_put = function(tree, u, d, delta_t, r, K, type){
  q = q_prob(r, delta_t, u, d)
  option_tree = matrix(0, nrow = nrow(tree), ncol = ncol(tree))
  
  if(type == 'put'){
    option_tree[nrow(option_tree), ] = pmax(K - tree[nrow(tree), ], 0)
  } else {
    option_tree[nrow(option_tree), ] = pmax(tree[nrow(tree), ] - K, 0)
  }
  
  for (i in (nrow(tree) - 1):1){
    for (j in 1:i){
      option_tree[i, j] = ((1 - q) * option_tree[i + 1, j] + q * option_tree[i + 1, j + 1]) / exp(r * delta_t)
    }
  }
  
  return(option_tree)
}
